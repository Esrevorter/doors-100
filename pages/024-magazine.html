Notes From the Tide Table

There is a notebook kept in a harbour shed in which volunteers have written the sea's mood, by hand, every morning since 1962. Calm. Choppy. "Politely indifferent." It is, I have come to believe, the best newspaper in Britain.

Slow living is sold to us as candles and linen. But the tide table knows the truth: slowness isn't aesthetic, it's attention. You cannot rush water. You can only show up at the right hour and be grateful.

Turn the page for three days of bread, a profile of the last night-train conductor, and an argument with a snail.
